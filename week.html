Three signals · through 2026-09-04

A Federal Production Credit Is Being Drafted
The Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act could reach the House the week of September 14, at 20 percent of U.S. labor costs. The MPA has an economic impact report due the same week and the DGA holds a studio side letter promising executive lobbying. Insiders told TheWrap mid-November is the earliest passage. (8/31, 9/1, 9/2, 9/4)
Potential Talent Implication: If above-the-line pay counts toward the credit without a per-person cap, star-driven shoots could return to the U.S., which may give talent more say over where a production is based.
Paramount Has 26 Days Before the Money Starts Leaving
The $7 million a day owed to Warner shareholders starts October 1. Ellison has threatened to move Paramount out of California if the twelve state attorneys general do not settle by then. The bond hearing is September 24, the trial starts March 2, and the merger agreement expires June 4. (8/31, 9/1, 9/3, 9/4)
Potential Talent Implication: Deals that need Paramount or Warner corporate approval could get harder to close after October 1, so talent with pending business there may want to close now or price in the delay.
Two Films Are Holding Up the International Box Office
The UK took $161.3 million in August, its best since 2018, on two July releases. France set an all-time August record. Spider-Man: Brand New Day and The Odyssey account for close to 32 percent of every U.S. ticket sold since the first Friday in May, and The Odyssey holds 39 Imax 70mm screens through September 30. (8/31, 9/3, 9/4)
Potential Talent Implication: Buyers can use these records to argue the theatrical market is back, but the strength sits in two event films, so talent on mid-size releases should not expect the same leverage on theatrical commitments.
