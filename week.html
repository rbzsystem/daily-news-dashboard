Guilds, CAA and WME All Told the OpenAI Court AI Training Already Has a Price
SAG-AFTRA, both Writers Guilds and the AFM filed on October 29 against fair use for AI training in The New York Times case against OpenAI. CAA and stars followed on October 30, and WME filed the same day, saying training should be licensed and citing 28 paid data deals. The U.S. government backs OpenAI. (11/2, 10/30, 10/29)
Potential Talent Implication: The guilds and the biggest agencies are now arguing the same thing in one courtroom, and a fair use ruling for OpenAI may leave the AI licensing terms they negotiated with little to license.
California Proposed Putting Star and Director Pay Inside Its Film Credit
Gavin Newsom proposed on November 2 raising California's film and TV credit from $750 million to at least $1 billion a year, making above-the-line pay eligible within caps, reserving $100 million for independents and extending the program to 2040. The next governor and the Legislature would have to pass it. (11/2, 10/29)
Potential Talent Implication: California's credit has never covered what actors and directors are paid, and if that changes, a large above-the-line budget could stop being a reason to shoot elsewhere.
Disney and Skydance Are Cutting Costs and Naming Content as What Gets Paid
Bloomberg reported Disney's fourth layoff round of 2026 will reach thousands in the TV networks and film studio, with some managers given 20 percent savings targets. The Ankler's sources say Skydance is weighing dropping Big Ten football, NASCAR and the French Open as an NFL renewal and $80 billion of debt loom. (11/3, 11/2)
Potential Talent Implication: Both companies say content is what the savings protect, but the people who greenlight it are the ones being cut, and CBS scripted volume is now named as one way to pay for football.
