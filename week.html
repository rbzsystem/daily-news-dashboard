Netflix Cut Its Ad Forecast After Replacing Its Content and Film Chiefs
Netflix laid off about 1,000 people on October 14, replaced Bela Bajaria with Peter Rice on October 15 and fired Dan Lin on October 16. On October 20 it reported third-quarter revenue up 11.8 percent, its slowest growth in three years, and cut its 2026 advertising forecast from $3 billion to about $2.7 billion. (10/20, 10/16, 10/15, 10/14)
Potential Talent Implication: Netflix told investors the United States needs better execution and has started publishing grosses on its own films, a number a filmmaker can now cite when negotiating a release plan.
Agencies Want Streamer Shows Back in Producers' Hands After Three Years
Puck reported the American Entertainment Alliance plan on October 20: streamers with 10 million U.S. subscribers would spend half of original budgets with independent producers, and exclusivity would end after three years for films and at most five for series, with no matching right. (10/20, 10/15)
Potential Talent Implication: UTA's Rich Shuter concedes streamers would pay lower license fees under the plan, and the producer would get back a library to license again.
Legendary Reportedly Bid for Lionsgate and Its 20,000-Title Library
Bloomberg reported on October 20 that Legendary, owned by Apollo and management, submitted an offer for Lionsgate, and Lionsgate shares rose more than 15 percent to above $12. Legendary releases through Paramount and would gain a distribution arm and a library that includes John Wick, The Hunger Games and Twilight. (10/20)
Potential Talent Implication: If a sale happens, anyone with backend or reversion rights in the Lionsgate library gets a new counterparty, and the 3 Arts management company Lionsgate owns changes hands with it.
