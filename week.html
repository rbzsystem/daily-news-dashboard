The Paramount Settlement Fixes a 45-Day Theatrical Window for Five Years
The consent decree with 12 state attorneys general sets a 45-day theatrical window and no streaming release before 90 days, alongside a floor of 30 films a year rising to 32. A shortfall costs $30 million per film into the Hollywood guilds' health and retirement plans. No cable channels have to be sold. (9/23, 9/22, 9/21, 9/12, 9/11)
Potential Talent Implication: A theatrical window that used to be studio policy is now a number a court can enforce, which gives release bonuses and every term defined by a theatrical run a fixed reference for five years.
Paramount Is Borrowing Another $7.5 Billion to Buy Warner
Paramount launched a $7.5 billion secured term loan on September 24 and said it plans about $44.4 billion more in secured debt to pay for Warner Bros. Discovery. The combined company carries roughly $80 billion of debt against a $6 billion savings target, and Warner showrunners are already leaving. (9/24, 9/23, 9/22)
Potential Talent Implication: Budgets at the combined studio after the close may be set by what remains of about $3 billion a year in free cash flow once interest is paid, which puts the key-person and change-of-control terms in an overall deal ahead of the guarantee.
The Federal Film Credit Bill Counts Star Salaries at 20 Percent
Senators Tim Scott and Adam Schiff introduced the bill on September 24: 20 percent of U.S. production compensation, excluding residuals, with 5 percent uplifts to 30 percent for independents and disaster-area shoots, Los Angeles included. TheWrap reports above-the-line pay counts. Schiff is aiming for the lame-duck session. (9/24, 9/23, 9/21)
Potential Talent Implication: If the bill passes, a producer earns back a fifth of a star's or director's U.S. salary, the same above-the-line credit TheWrap says drew Barbie and Deadpool & Wolverine to shoot abroad.
