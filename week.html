Guilds, CAA and Stars Told the OpenAI Court AI Training Already Has a Price
On October 29 SAG-AFTRA, both Writers Guilds and the AFM asked the court in The New York Times case against OpenAI to reject fair use for AI training. A day later CAA and the Human Consent Foundation filed a brief signed by stars including Robert De Niro, arguing licensing already works at scale. The U.S. government backs OpenAI. (10/30, 10/29)
Potential Talent Implication: If the court rules that training is fair use, the AI licensing terms the guilds and talent negotiated may have nothing left to license.
Netflix Fired Its Content and Film Chiefs After Filmmakers Walked
Ted Sarandos fired Bela Bajaria on October 14 and Dan Lin two days later. Bloomberg reports the Duffer Brothers, Noah Baumbach and Shawn Levy had left, David Fincher did not renew, and partners Lin turned down went to Sarandos. Netflix also cut its ad forecast to about $2.7 billion. Peter Rice starts October 26. (10/26, 10/21, 10/20)
Potential Talent Implication: Agents Bryan Lourd and Ari Emanuel are already lobbying Peter Rice, and the budget fights Dan Lin had with Kathryn Bigelow and Greta Gerwig may be open again for every director with a Netflix film in development.
A Star's Salary Counts Toward the Film Credit in New York, Not California
Congress returns November 9 aiming to pass the federal film credit before a December 18 Senate adjournment. New York and New Jersey count above-the-line pay toward their credits; California's $750 million program does not. Head Gear is bringing $120 million to fund the uncovered half of U.S. indie budgets. (10/28, 10/27)
Potential Talent Implication: Where a star's fee counts toward the credit it lowers the picture's net cost, which makes the cast attachment part of the location decision for studio films returning to the U.S.
