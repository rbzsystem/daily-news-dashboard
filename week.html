Paramount and Warner Bros. Become Skydance, and the Studio's Film Chiefs Are Out
David Ellison named the newly merged Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery "Skydance" Friday, ahead of a debt-heavy $110 billion close expected Oct. 6. The same week, Warner Bros. chairs Michael De Luca and Pam Abdy learned from news reports, not a call from Ellison, that Josh Greenstein and Dana Goldberg will run the studio instead. (10/2, 10/1)
Potential Talent Implication: A century-old studio just changed its name and its leadership in the same week, and every deal attached to the old regime, De Luca and Abdy's included, now has to be renegotiated with people who did not make it.
Universal Won Taylor Sheridan by Fixing the Weak Clause in His Paramount Deal
NBCUniversal's Donna Langley said she pulled Taylor Sheridan from Paramount after a decade by upgrading the film side of his deal, since his Paramount film pact was only a weaker first-look arrangement. On Oct. 1, 101 Studios, which came to NBCUniversal alongside Sheridan, set its first project there: Eddie Murphy's first series. (10/1, 10/3)
Potential Talent Implication: Langley outbid Paramount only on the one clause in Sheridan's deal with the least in it, and a rival offer may land hardest when it targets the weakest part of a creator's current deal.
Barbie 2 Is Stuck Because CAA Wants a Bigger Check
CAA's Bryan Lourd sought fees for Greta Gerwig, Noah Baumbach, Margot Robbie and Ryan Gosling so high that a Barbie sequel's breakeven topped $700 million, and Warner Bros. let the talks collapse rather than pay, according to Puck. (10/2)
Potential Talent Implication: Lourd is betting a merged, better-funded Skydance will pay the price he already named rather than relitigate it, and a representative's leverage over a stalled deal only grows once the buyer across the table changes.
