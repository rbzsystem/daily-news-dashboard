Paramount's Merger Delay Now Has a Price and a Date
A $7 million-a-day fee to Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders starts October 1, more than $635 million a quarter. Paramount wants 12 state attorneys general and the Writers Guild to post a $1.88 billion bond; they countered with $10,000. The bond hearing is September 24 and trial is March 2. (9/12, 9/11, 9/8)
Potential Talent Implication: Every project at Paramount now sits behind a dated corporate decision, which puts the weight on start-date guarantees and pay-or-play triggers until March.
Renewal Decisions Are Running on Cost, Not Acclaim
Prime Video canceled Spider-Noir days before it won five Creative Arts Emmys, with sources citing its price tag. Marvel reversed a second season of Wonder Man after a lead-actor nomination. HBO's Lanterns drew 10 million global viewers on August 30 and has no renewal. Disney opened Kimmel's renewal talks by asking for cuts. (9/12, 9/11, 9/4)
Potential Talent Implication: Awards and viewership held none of these shows, so the renewal protection worth negotiating is the episodic cost ceiling and a guaranteed second-season order.
The Material Is Originating Outside the Studios
CAA's Roeg Sutherland told TIFF's new market that studios are not developing as they used to and that the ideas talent wants are not coming through traditional routes. Searchlight's Matthew Greenfield said YouTube now supplies storytellers who built their own audiences. Twelve bidders chased The Mandela Catalogue, whose creator directs. (9/12, 9/11)
Potential Talent Implication: The person holding the underlying material sets the terms of the package, which moves leverage from the development deal to the option and the attachment.
