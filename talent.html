The Federal Film Credit Bill Counts Star Salaries Toward the 20 Percent 9/24

A bipartisan group introduced the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act on Thursday. It pays a 20% credit on compensation tied to a production, excluding residuals, and TheWrap reports that above-the-line salaries count, as they do in the UK and Canada. Uplifts of 5% each, to a 30% ceiling, go to shoots in rural opportunity zones or federal disaster areas (Los Angeles County qualifies until 2030), independent productions, shoots across 10 or more states, and studios that raise domestic output. Credits stack on state incentives. Congress is out until after the midterms; Schiff is aiming for the lame-duck session.
Potential Talent Implication: If the bill passes, a producer earns back 20 percent of star and director salaries, and up to 30 percent on an independent shot in Los Angeles, the same above-the-line credit that sent Barbie and Deadpool & Wolverine to shoot abroad.
