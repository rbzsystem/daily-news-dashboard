Anime Production Houses Now Take Equity in Their Shows to Share in $16 Billion of Overseas Sales

Japan's anime industry grew 15.8% to $28.18 billion in 2025, according to the Association of Japanese Animations (AJA), presented at TIFFCOM. Overseas revenue rose 17% to $16.1 billion, about 57% of the total; domestic revenue rose just over 14% to $12 billion. Revenue reaching production companies, as distinct from the production committees (the investor groups that finance and own most anime), rose 8% to $3.2 billion. AJA's Masahiro Hasegawa said more production companies now take an equity position in their works to keep the IP and share in overseas revenue.
Potential Talent Implication: The companies that make anime collect about $3.2 billion of a $28 billion business, and the route to a larger share is equity in the title itself, which carries the overseas revenue a production fee never touches.

Marcus Says an Early Home Release Wins Few Extra Rentals and Costs Box Office

Marcus Corp. chairman and CEO Greg Marcus said on the company's third-quarter call and to Deadline that the propensity to buy a $20 to $25 premium home rental is about the same from week two through week eight. His argument: studios lose few rentals by waiting and gain theatrical box office. He also asked studios to hold "available at home" marketing until a film's exclusive run ends. Universal has promised a 45-day exclusive window for major releases from 2027, and Skydance has committed to 45-day and 90-day windows with limits on home-viewing marketing.
Potential Talent Implication: If rentals hold at week eight, an early home release mainly moves money out of the theatrical gross, which is the number most backend and box office bonuses are paid on.
