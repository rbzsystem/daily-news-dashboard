Meta rebutted the same night and defended its opt-out design. This is the second likeness confrontation between a top agency and a Big Tech generative-AI launch in under a year, after OpenAI's Sora.
Key Takeaways
  • AI Consent: The AI-likeness battle is not about banning the tools, it is about who carries the burden of consent.
  • Platform Leverage: Meta held the opt-out line on launch night, so shifting the default will take legislative or collective leverage, not press releases.

Why It's Happening

Meta defending opt-out while touting "guardrails" signals it sees real engagement value in Muse Image that it will not trade away voluntarily. If the pressure eventually forces consent infrastructure for verified talent accounts, that creates a genuinely new deal category: a platform-side likeness-licensing agreement.
The Facts
  • A major Hollywood talent agency publicly demanded Wednesday night that Meta make privacy protection the DEFAULT setting on its newly launched Muse Image AI tool, not an opt-out a user must find and activate.   • The agency's roster includes major film stars and digital-first talent whose public accounts are exposed to the tool by default.
  • The agency's statement: "No one's name, image, likeness, voice, or creative work should be used by any third party, including AI models, without clear, documented consent... Private accounts and those belonging to users under 18 are automatically excluded and adult users with public accounts can opt out with just a couple clicks.
